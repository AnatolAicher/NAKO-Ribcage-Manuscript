NAKO Ribcage SSM

Statistical and surface-based shape modelling of the human rib cage

This site is the interactive companion to the manuscript How Sex, Age, Adiposity, and Smoking Shape the Human Rib Cage: Evidence from 26,275 Whole-Body MRIs across the German National Cohort (NAKO). It hosts the full text and supplement with interactive versions of every figure, a browser-based viewer for the statistical shape model, and pointers to the source code and the released model.

Summary

We built a surface-based statistical shape model (SSM) of the complete 24-rib cage from 26,275 standardised whole-body MRI scans of adults aged 19–74 years in the population-based German National Cohort (NAKO). Ribs were segmented with a deep-learning pipeline, reconstructed as per-rib surface meshes, brought into dense vertex-wise correspondence by Gaussian-process morphable registration, and summarised by generalised Procrustes analysis and principal component analysis (PCA). Fourteen per-rib geometric descriptors link the abstract PCA modes to named shape features, and associations with sex, age, body size and composition, and smoking exposure were estimated by multivariable regression with false-discovery-rate control.

Shape variation is strongly concentrated: 28 modes capture 95% of the total variance, and the first three alone account for 69.4% (PC1 42.6%, PC2 16.3%, PC3 10.5%). They admit consistent anatomical readings – a sexually dimorphic axis (PC1; Cohen’s d = 2.52 between the sexes), a slender-versus-stout body-habitus contrast (PC2), and a free-rib-size axis at ribs 11–12 (PC3). Body mass and body-fat percentage are the dominant modifiable correlates of rib-cage shape, whereas the association with cumulative smoking exposure is comparatively small.
